    Episode guide

    Season 1 · The Proposal Circles

    Season 1 of The Pit Theater follows the Open Source Orchestra — a circle of musicians who play open rooms at Ethereum gatherings, whoever shows up, every contribution equal — around Devcon 8 in Mumbai, as its people ask what should outlast the gig. The pilot is the first open recording, one week before the doors opened: seven voices planning a music sanctuary and then playing for each other. Five circles follow — the Open Source Orchestra DAO proposal meetings. The first and second gather friends from Chiang Mai to Leeds, Los Angeles, Goa and Mumbai around retroactive thanks and receipts before rewards. The third adds Barcelona and asks how to steward an open room without policing it. The fourth is two friends taking the word "token" apart until only a thank-you is left. The fifth plants a memory garden: one beautiful moment of music that made you feel you belonged. No token is launched, no treasury exists, no vote is taken — the DAO stays a proposal, and every page says so. The road leads to Devcon 9 in Lisbon, 12 to 14 October 2027. Every episode is published with the consent of everyone in it, first names only, takedown first.

    A field guide for new viewers

    How to watch. Start anywhere; each episode stands alone, but they are best in order: the pilot, then the five circles. Every episode page carries the film, a transcript that follows along as it plays, a TV-guide blurb, a recap of what the circle talked about, five quotes with timestamps, chapters you can jump to, a short "why this matters" for newcomers and a glossary. Captions can be turned on; the transcripts are public and searchable, and the audio is available in any podcast app through the feed. Short on time? Read the recap, then jump by chapter. Treat the transcripts as honest but imperfect: four were made by a local speech model and one from live captions by a bot; speaker labels are editorial, and a name is attached to a line only when the recording itself makes it clear who is speaking.

    How to read what you hear. Everything in these circles is a conversation, not a decision. When someone says "we should", it is an idea. When Shaka says "the first proposals should be reimbursements with receipts", that is his proposal, offered to the circle. When Alaska offers a plaza in Barcelona or Ben offers to hunt for grants, those are personal offers. The Open Source Orchestra DAO is a proposal and a public community ledger; it is not an on-chain organisation, there is no token and no treasury, and nobody becomes a member by appearing in an episode. If a page ever reads as though something was decided, that is a mistake and you can tell us.

    How to join. The orchestra is whoever shows up: musicians, DJs, singers, developers, designers, stewards, sound people, translators, photographers. Open jams never need an application — you come and play. If you want a public artist profile and a place on the road to Lisbon, there is one door: https://opensourceorchestra.org/join/. A rotating circle of stewards reads every application. No wallet is required to join, and no meeting link is ever posted in public; the join page is the way in.

    How to record your own circle the consent-first way. Say on the recording that you are recording, and ask everyone whether it can be shared — then ask again before you publish, because a recording notice is not the same as publication consent. Use first names only in anything public. Remove surnames, emails, phone numbers and links that people pasted. Keep the raw file private and publish a transcript people can read and correct. Label anything a machine wrote as written by a machine. Publish a takedown door before you publish the recording, and honour every request first and ask questions second. The protocol we follow, step by step, is at https://publicinform.com/#consent. Fork it.

    What we stand on.     Join · listen · record your own

    Come as you are. Open jams at any Ethereum gathering where the orchestra plays need no application — walk up, pick an instrument or a mic, and play. If you want a public artist profile and a place on the road to Devcon 9 in Lisbon (12 to 14 October 2027), apply at https://opensourceorchestra.org/join/ — open to musicians, DJs, singers, developers, designers, stewards, sound people, translators and photographers. A rotating circle of stewards reads every application. No wallet, no ticket, no fee. The proposal circles are open calls too; the join page is the only door to them, because we never post meeting links in public. Every contribution is equal, one voice each.
